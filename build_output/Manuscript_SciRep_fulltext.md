## Abstract

IgA nephropathy (IgAN) is initiated by galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1), produced by the O-glycosylation machinery of B cells and plasma cells. Whether genetic and epigenetic variation acting on this machinery controls Gd-IgA1 levels, IgAN susceptibility, or both, and in which cell type, is unresolved. We assembled a multi-omics causal-inference framework from public data—disease transcriptomes, cell-type-resolved expression quantitative trait loci, IgAN and Gd-IgA1 genome-wide association summary statistics, whole-blood methylation quantitative trait loci, population allele frequencies and IgAN peripheral-blood single-cell transcriptomes—and interrogated the five-gene biosynthesis axis across nine evidence layers. The axis was down-regulated in IgAN blood but directionally separated in kidney. The C1GALT1 lead rs13226913 was a naive B-cell-enriched cis-eQTL. Of 25 planned gene-by-context colocalisation tests, 14 were computable and none supported a shared causal variant with IgAN (maximum shared-variant posterior 0.031; ≤1.3% power at the observed effect size), whereas two leads reached genome-wide significance for Gd-IgA1 in Han Chinese. No axis gene was associated with IgAN susceptibility. Single-cell upstream-regulator inference was negative, yet the C1GALT1C1 promoter is dense in methylation-blockable Sp/KLF motifs. The framework places the axis at the initiating hit of the multi-hit model and identifies reversible acquired epigenetic control as the principal window for Gd-IgA1-lowering intervention.

## Keywords

IgA nephropathy; galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1); C1GALT1; cell-type-specific eQTL; colocalisation; East Asian populations

## Introduction

IgA nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide and a leading cause of progressive kidney disease [1]. The disease burden is particularly high in East Asian populations, where the incidence of IgAN is estimated at approximately 45 cases per million population per year (pmp/y) in Japan, compared with about 31 pmp/y in a French cohort [2]. Despite this high prevalence, the pathogenesis remains complex. The prevailing framework is the multi-hit model, in which elevated circulating galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) constitutes the initiating hit of the disease cascade [3]. Gd-IgA1 is produced by B cells due to incomplete O-glycosylation in the IgA1 hinge region. This abnormal glycan structure acts as a neo-epitope, triggering the production of autoantibodies and the formation of immune complexes that deposit in the glomeruli [4-5]. As Gd-IgA1 production is intrinsic to B cells and regulated by common genetic variants, the genetic and epigenetic control of this biosynthetic pathway likely determines an individual's Gd-IgA1 levels and susceptibility to the initial hit of IgAN.
Genetic studies of this axis have so far proceeded at two disconnected levels. Quantitative genome-wide association studies (GWAS) of circulating Gd-IgA1 have mapped common variants at C1GALT1 in European and Chinese cohorts and at GALNT12 in Han Chinese patients [6, 7, 8]. However, the expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) resources conventionally used to interpret such loci are predominantly derived from bulk tissues, in which cell-type composition averages away regulatory effects restricted to a single lineage [9]. Immune gene regulation is strongly cell-type-specific: in the OneK1K single-cell cohort, most cis-eQTLs detected across 14 immune cell types show allelic effects restricted to specific cell types, and cis-regulation in B cells changes dynamically along the naive-to-memory transition [10]. Because Gd-IgA1 is synthesised by a dedicated B-cell and plasma-cell compartment, whether the Gd-IgA1-associated variants act through B-cell-specific cis-regulation—and through which B-cell state—cannot be resolved from bulk data and has not been systematically tested. Defining this cellular context is a prerequisite for assigning the genetic signal of the axis to the production phenotype (Gd-IgA1 quantity) rather than to disease susceptibility itself. At the population level, a second gap compounds this. The only two genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 associations reported in East Asian individuals both come from Han Chinese patients with IgAN [8], whereas trans-ancestry IgAN susceptibility GWAS have not implicated the same loci [11]; whether the axis is directionally consistent across ancestries—and whether allele-frequency differences are compatible with a shared causal role—remains untested within a single framework. This carries direct clinical weight, since East Asian populations bear the highest disease burden [1] and the value of Gd-IgA1-lowering strategies depends on the axis behaving similarly in the populations most likely to benefit.

Here we establish a multi-omics causal-inference framework that addresses the cell-type and population gaps jointly. We integrate publicly available disease transcriptomes, cell-type-resolved eQTL maps, IgAN and Gd-IgA1 GWAS summary statistics, whole-blood methylation QTLs (mQTLs), population allele frequencies, and single-cell transcriptomes for cell-state and upstream-regulator inference, and interrogate the Gd-IgA1 biosynthesis axis across nine sequential evidence layers, the last two of which resolve the cell state in which the axis operates and probe the axis's upstream transcriptional regulation at single-cell resolution. The aim of this study was to determine whether the published Gd-IgA1 lead variants act through B-cell-specific cis-regulation of the biosynthesis axis (Q1), whether the axis is subject to transcription-coupled epigenetic control downstream of its genetic signal (Q2), whether the resulting signal contributes to Gd-IgA1 quantity or extends to IgAN susceptibility (Q3), and whether the effect is population-specific (Q4). We therefore resolve the cell type in which the lead variants act, relate the genetic-to-transcriptional step to gene-body CpG methylation, test whether the axis signal is shared with IgAN susceptibility, and assess the population specificity of the effect, so as to identify the regulatory layer at which Gd-IgA1-lowering interventions would be most tractable.

## Results

### The O-glycosylation biosynthesis axis is attenuated in peripheral blood but directionally separated in kidney
We first asked whether transcription of the IgA1 O-glycosylation machinery is altered in IgAN (Fig. 1b). In peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs; GSE73953), all five axis genes exhibited lower expression levels in patients relative to the pooled healthy control: C1GALT1 (log2 fold change [logFC] = −1.73), C1GALT1C1 (logFC = −2.46), ST6GALNAC2 (logFC = −2.16), GALNT2 (logFC = −4.43) and GALNT12 (logFC = −2.12) (Fig. 1b; Table S17). Because this series pools only two healthy controls, the PBMC finding is a directional signal; its consistency across all five genes nevertheless supports reduced galactosylation capacity in the circulating B-cell/plasma-cell compartment of patients (per-dataset box plots in Supplementary Fig. S1).
Kidney transcriptomes showed a directionally different pattern. In whole kidney-biopsy tissue (GSE115857, DASL platform), GALNT2 (logFC = +0.68, P = 0.004) and ST6GALNAC2 (logFC = +0.39, P = 0.003) were significantly up-regulated in IgAN, whereas C1GALT1 showed no detectable change. The DASL platform of this series has a narrow dynamic range that attenuates its measurements in both directions; we therefore read the whole-kidney series as directional and do not treat either its nulls or its significant results as platform-free, and no platform-specific mechanism is invoked selectively for individual genes. In microdissected glomeruli (GSE93798), C1GALT1 was modestly down-regulated (logFC = −0.24, P = 3.4 × 10⁻⁵), while C1GALT1C1 (logFC = +0.20, P = 0.003) and GALNT2 (logFC = +0.18, P = 0.002) were up-regulated (Fig. 1b; Table S17). The absence of a coordinated down-regulation of the axis within the kidney, in contrast to the peripheral compartment, is consistent with the kidney being a site of Gd-IgA1 deposition rather than a major site of its biosynthesis, and supports a peripheral origin of the aberrantly glycosylated IgA1. Quantifying the compartment contrast: all five axis genes were down-regulated in the PBMC series (5 of 5), whereas in the two kidney datasets only one of the ten gene-series measurements was significantly down-regulated (C1GALT1 in microdissected glomeruli) and four were significantly up-regulated (GALNT2 and ST6GALNAC2 in whole kidney; GALNT2 and C1GALT1C1 in glomeruli), the remaining five measurements being non-significant (all |logFC| ≤ 0.32, with C1GALT1 in whole kidney at logFC = 0.00); the two compartments therefore differ in the sign of the axis response and not merely in its magnitude.
### C1GALT1 cis-regulation is enriched in naive B cells: cell-type-specific eQTL context of the axis
Bulk PBMC data cannot resolve the cell type that carries the regulatory signal (Q1). We therefore mapped cis-eQTL evidence for each axis gene across cell-type-resolved and tissue contexts (Fig. 2a; Supplementary Table S1). In the OneK1K single-cell panel, C1GALT1 showed a measurable naive-B-cell cis-eQTL (gene-level permutation P [p_perm] = 0.018), and GALNT2 was significantly cis-regulated in naive B cells (gene-level permutation P = 1.0 × 10⁻³, the permutation floor; nominal P = 1.6 × 10⁻⁶). Three features of context non-measurability were recorded as genuine data properties rather than technical failures: GALNT12 and ST6GALNAC2 are expressed at very low levels in B-cell subtypes and are filtered by the discovery pipeline; C1GALT1C1 resides on chromosome X, which is not represented in the OneK1K eQTL release; and a given gene could be measurable in bulk tissue (whole blood, kidney cortex) but not in purified B-cell states.
We next tested whether the four published Gd-IgA1 lead variants act as cell-type-specific cis-eQTLs for the axis (Fig. 2b–c; Supplementary Figs. S2 and S3; Table S18). The C1GALT1 lead rs13226913 showed a nominal cis-eQTL for C1GALT1 in naive B cells that remained below the genome-wide threshold (P = 9.36 × 10⁻⁶; β = +0.17), was attenuated in memory (P = 0.056) and intermediate (P = 0.40) B cells, and was corroborated in GTEx v10 whole blood (P = 5.6 × 10⁻¹²) and kidney cortex (P = 1.9 × 10⁻⁵). The second C1GALT1 lead, rs10238682, likewise showed naive-B-cell cis-regulation (P = 1.16 × 10⁻⁴) and a strong kidney-cortex eQTL (P = 3.0 × 10⁻¹¹). By contrast, the remaining two leads showed no significant cis-eQTL in the GTEx v10 contexts in which their genes were measurable: the GALNT12 lead rs7856182 (P = 0.20 and 0.23 in whole blood and kidney cortex, respectively) and the X-chromosomal C1GALT1C1 lead rs5910940 (P = 0.31 and 0.52 in the same tissues). C1GALT1C1—the node nominated for intervention below—therefore carries no significant cis-eQTL evidence in any context in which it is testable, and it is absent altogether from the OneK1K panel and from the IgAN meta-analysis. Thus, for the C1GALT1 locus, the Gd-IgA1 genetic signal is localised to naive-B-cell cis-regulation—the state in which the effect is strongest—identifying the cellular context in which the axis is genetically controlled. A formal test on the uniformly recomputed per-subset effects confirmed that the effect of rs13226913 is gated by B-cell state (Cochran's Q = 6.15, df = 2, P = 0.046; I² = 67.5%) and that the naive-versus-antigen-experienced contrast is significant (weighted meta-regression β = −0.116, P = 0.018; Supplementary Table S11 and Fig. S7). The gating is not a monotonic decline along maturation: the effect drops sharply in the intermediate state (rs13226913 β = 0.035, P = 0.40) and partially recovers in the memory state (β = 0.081, P = 0.056), so we describe the axis as strongest in the naive state and attenuated in the antigen-experienced states rather than as a gradient along the naive-to-memory trajectory.
### The genetic signal of the axis is trait-specific for Gd-IgA1 and shows no shared structure with IgAN susceptibility
To assign the axis signal to the production phenotype or to disease susceptibility, we performed colocalisation between the axis cis-eQTLs and IgAN (GCST90018866; Fig. 3a–c; Table S19; Q3). Of the 25 planned gene-by-context comparisons (five genes × five contexts), 14 were computable: C1GALT1C1 is absent from the IgAN meta-analysis (chromosome X), and GALNT12 and ST6GALNAC2 lack B-cell eQTL records. No comparison supported a shared causal variant: the posterior probability of a shared causal variant (PPH4; PPH0–PPH4 denote the five coloc hypotheses, with PPH1 an eQTL signal without a disease signal and PPH3 distinct causal variants) did not reach 0.5 in any of the 14 tests (maximum 0.031), and posterior mass concentrated on PPH1 in the comparisons with the strongest cis-eQTL evidence (PPH1 = 0.79–0.91; Table S19; Supplementary Tables S2a and S2b). Signal-level re-testing with the official SuSiE credible sets (seven signal-level tests across four comparisons) reproduced this null (maximum PPH4 = 0.031; Supplementary Table S3), and a sensitivity analysis at p12 = 5 × 10⁻⁶ did not change the conclusion. A simulation-based power analysis calibrated to the observed standard errors shows that this null excludes only large shared effects: for a shared variant with a per-allele log-OR at the upper end of the effects observed at this locus (0.02), power to reach PPH4 ≥ 0.8 was ≤ 1.3% in every context, and 80% power required a log-OR of ≈ 0.08 (OR ≈ 1.08) or larger (Supplementary Fig. S8 and Table S16a). An unweighted allelic score of the three autosomal leads against IgAN was likewise null (β = 0.0039, SE = 0.025, P = 0.876). Because rs13226913 and rs10238682 lie in linkage disequilibrium, this nominal P value rests on an independence assumption that the data violate; the score is therefore reported strictly as an informal direction-consistency summary, without inferential weight, and not as an instrument-variable analysis.
The same axis signal is, however, strongly associated with the production phenotype. The two leads that reached genome-wide significance for Gd-IgA1 in Han Chinese patients—rs10238682 (P = 1.2 × 10⁻⁹) and rs7856182 (P = 2.4 × 10⁻⁹)—are the very variants for which we find no shared structure with IgAN, and the remaining leads also did not show association with IgAN susceptibility (rs13226913: P = 0.284 in IgAN; rs5910940: not testable in the IgAN meta-analysis). These results indicate that the axis signal is specific to the glycan trait rather than to antibody quantity: none of the 20 genome-wide significant loci for serum total IgA levels [29] maps to the five axis-gene regions. In a direct look-up, none of the three testable axis leads was associated with serum total IgA (P = 0.917, 0.216 and 0.170 for rs13226913, rs10238682 and rs7856182, respectively), and no variant in any of the four testable axis-gene regions reached genome-wide significance (smallest regional P = 5.2 × 10⁻⁵ at C1GALT1; Supplementary Table S15). The genetic evidence of the axis therefore resolves into a strong determinant of Gd-IgA1 quantity and no shared causal variant detectable with IgAN susceptibility.
### Gene-body CpG methylation marks transcriptional coupling without mediating IgAN risk
To test the epigenetic layer (Fig. 4a–d; Q2), we queried whole-blood mQTLs (GoDMC) for the two interpretable autosomal C1GALT1 leads. Five CpGs showed significant cis-mQTL associations (P < 5 × 10⁻⁸): four map to the C1GALT1 gene body (+25.7, +28.3, +65.0 and +72.2 kb from the transcription start site [TSS]) and one to the 3′-flanking region (+94.9 kb); none lies within the promoter (TSS ± 1.5 kb) (Fig. 4a; Table S20). CpG-to-IgAN Mendelian randomization was uniformly null: Wald-ratio estimates using the lead variants or the top cis-sentinel variant of each CpG, and clumped inverse-variance weighting (IVW) where at least three independent instruments were available, all gave P > 0.05, the smallest being the clumped-IVW estimate for cg04827551 (β = −0.057, P = 0.064) (Fig. 4b; Supplementary Tables S5a–S5d). Colocalisation of each mQTL with IgAN assigned essentially all posterior mass to PPH1 (0.984–0.989) with PPH4 ≤ 0.014, indicating that germline methylation at these CpGs does not share a causal variant with IgAN risk (Fig. 4c).
Colocalisation of the mQTLs with the C1GALT1 cis-eQTL resolved the role of these marks. In whole blood and kidney cortex, the methylation and expression signals of four of the five CpGs coexisted in the same region as distinct variants (PPH3 ≈ 1.0), whereas for cg17994788 the posterior favoured a shared causal variant in whole blood (PPH4 = 0.62) and kidney cortex (PPH4 = 0.97); in naive B cells, cg17994788 and cg16101574 showed PPH4 of 0.79 and 0.82, respectively. Under the principal lead rs13226913, allelic effects on methylation and expression were direction-consistent for four of the five CpGs (higher methylation coupled to higher expression), whereas cg04827551—the CpG with the smallest, still non-significant, MR P value—showed the opposite coupling. Together, these results identify the significant CpGs as transcription-coupled gene-body methylation marks rather than causal promoter switches, and provide no support for a germline methylation-mediated pathway to IgAN. Because the Han-Chinese Gd-IgA1 GWAS is released with P values only, the proportion of the genetic effect on Gd-IgA1 mediated by methylation could not be estimated; the mediation component is therefore concluded qualitatively from the strongly positive input layer, the doubly null mediation layer and direction consistency, pending reanalysis when full-effect-size data become available (see Limitations).
### Genetic perturbations of the biosynthesis axis do not drive IgAN susceptibility
To define the boundary of the axis's disease contribution (Q3), we examined the four published leads and the cis-eQTL regions directly in the IgAN meta-analysis. The three autosomal leads showed no association with IgAN (rs13226913: P = 0.284; rs10238682: P = 0.222; rs7856182: P = 0.100), and the X-chromosomal lead rs5910940 could not be evaluated because the meta-analysis does not include chromosome X. Region-level lookup of the top cis-eQTL variant of each axis gene across the pre-specified primary contexts (10 gene–context pairs) likewise identified no IgAN association approaching genome-wide significance; the smallest regional P value was 9.4 × 10⁻⁶ at the GALNT12 locus, still approximately two orders of magnitude above the genome-wide significance threshold (complete lookup in Supplementary Tables S4a and S4b). In an ancestry-matched Asian-only IgAN meta-analysis (4,590 biopsy-diagnosed cases and 7,573 controls), none of the three testable autosomal leads reached genome-wide significance (rs13226913: P = 0.077; rs10238682: P = 0.040; rs7856182: P = 0.394); all three betas were positive, with the Gd-IgA1-increasing allele as the effect allele, so, post hoc, the point estimates are directionally concordant with the axis but not statistically supported (reported as a descriptive consistency check rather than a pre-specified directional test); the nominal signal at rs10238682 does not survive Bonferroni correction for the three look-ups (adjusted P = 0.12) and lies more than six orders of magnitude above the genome-wide threshold, and rs5910940 remains untestable because chromosome X is absent from this meta-analysis as well (Supplementary Table S14). These data delimit the contribution of the biosynthesis axis: common germline variation in the axis affects the production phenotype (Gd-IgA1 quantity) but does not measurably drive IgAN susceptibility. Under the multi-hit model, this indicates that the first hit (excess Gd-IgA1) is genetically separable from the downstream hits—mucosal immunity, complement and other amplifiers—that convert the glycan trait into disease risk.
### The two genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 associations were discovered in Han Chinese, with marked East Asian–European differentiation at C1GALT1
Both genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 associations in our lead panel were first identified in Han Chinese patients with IgAN, and both are silent in the IgAN meta-analysis (rs10238682: Gd-IgA1 P = 1.2 × 10⁻⁹ vs IgAN P = 0.222; rs7856182: 2.4 × 10⁻⁹ vs 0.100) (Fig. 5a–b; Table S21; Q4). Allele frequencies in gnomAD v4 differed markedly between East Asian (EAS) and non-Finnish European (NFE) populations for the C1GALT1 leads: the rs13226913-C allele approaches fixation in EAS (0.926 vs 0.420 in NFE), and the rs10238682-G allele is approximately 2.6-fold more frequent in EAS (0.522 vs 0.204). At this locus the alleles that are more common in East Asians are the Gd-IgA1-lowering alleles, so the corresponding Gd-IgA1-increasing alleles reported by the source studies (rs13226913-T [7]; rs10238682-A [8]) are less frequent in EAS (0.074 vs 0.580 and 0.478 vs 0.796, respectively); the East Asian differentiation of the locus therefore lies in the frequency of the non-risk allele rather than in enrichment of the risk allele; the high East Asian disease burden is therefore not explained by enrichment of the Gd-IgA1-increasing alleles at this locus. The GALNT12 lead rs7856182 was the least common in East Asia (0.025 vs 0.154 in NFE)—a discordant variant that we report as measured and do not generalise into a uniform "East Asian enrichment"; the X-chromosomal C1GALT1C1 lead had comparable frequencies across ancestries (0.546 vs 0.511). An ancestry-matched Chinese reference panel (Westlake Biobank for Chinese, WBBC; 4,480 individuals) reproduced these three estimates (rs13226913-C: 0.934; rs10238682-G: 0.529; rs7856182-T: 0.026), placing the C1GALT1 differentiation directly in the Chinese target population (Table S21; Supplementary Table S14). Formal trans-ancestry heterogeneity testing was not possible because the Han-Chinese Gd-IgA1 release contains P values only; the East-Asian-specific structure is instead supported by the convergence of the P-value distribution, the disease-negative outcome and the allele-frequency stratification. To set this ancestry-specific genetic structure in a regulatory context, we cross-referenced the axis with an independent East Asian eQTL resource (ImmuNexUT; 416 Japanese individuals; 28 purified immune-cell subsets [25]; Supplementary Tables S12 and S13). C1GALT1 was a significant eGene in all 28 subsets, including all five B-lineage subsets, whereas C1GALT1C1 was an eGene in none—mirroring its non-measurability in the European panels and confirming that the X-chromosomal node remains regulatory-untestable in both ancestries. The Han-Chinese Gd-IgA1 lead rs10238682 was itself a conditionally independent C1GALT1 signal in four of the five B-lineage subsets (naive, switched-memory, unswitched-memory and double-negative B cells; nominal slopes +0.23 to +0.33, all P < 1 × 10⁻⁶) and in 20 of the 28 subsets overall, with a direction concordant with the European naive-B-cell C1GALT1 eQTL; the second lead rs13226913 reached independent significance in only three of the 28 subsets, none of them B-lineage. The independent East Asian resource thus recapitulates the central regulatory feature of the axis—C1GALT1 cis-regulation across B-cell states—in a population matched to the cohort that produced the two genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 associations, while leaving the chaperone node untested. The East Asian dimension of the axis is therefore one of distinct genetic architecture rather than uniformly stronger signal: the two genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 associations in our panel were both discovered in Han Chinese patients—the population group with the highest IgAN burden—and the C1GALT1 locus shows marked East Asian–European allele-frequency differentiation, with the more frequent East Asian alleles being the Gd-IgA1-lowering ones. The disease-negative result holds in the IgAN meta-analysis, whose cases are nevertheless almost entirely of European ancestry and which is therefore not powered to exclude an axis contribution to IgAN risk in East Asians.
### The actionable window lies in acquired epigenetic control of the biosynthesis axis
Direct druggability of the axis was limited (Fig. 6a–c; Table S22). Pharos classified C1GALT1, C1GALT1C1 and ST6GALNAC2 as Tbio and GALNT12 as Tdark, with GALNT2 the only Tchem entry (three research-grade ligands; no approved drug for any axis member). Because enzyme active sites are therefore not a realistic intervention lever, we scored the transcriptomic/epigenetic intervention window of each gene with a prespecified framework (intervenability score = G + D + F − P; Methods; Supplementary Tables S6a and S6b). The chaperone C1GALT1C1 received the highest score (6.0), anchored by the only IgAN-context functional evidence among the five genes in our scoring—a single pediatric study in a B-cell line: cytokine-driven down-regulation of C1GALT1C1 (IL-17 [22]; IL-4 [23]) together with IL-4-driven hypermethylation of its promoter CpG island that is reversed by the demethylating agent 5-aza-2′-deoxycytidine [23]; in the IL-17 setting, 5-azacytidine likewise restored C1GALT1 and C1GALT1C1 expression [22]. C1GALT1 scored 4.5: it carries the strongest genetic anchor (naive-B-cell cis-eQTL plus a genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 association), but its methylation–expression coupling is correlational, cancer-type dependent and gene-body rather than promoter (no promoter CpG was significant in our own data), and its tumour-promoting activity in several solid cancers [19, 20, 21] cautions against systemic up-regulation. GALNT12, GALNT2 and ST6GALNAC2 each scored 2.5, with no IgAN-specific intervention evidence at present.
In this framework, the principal actionable window lies in acquired, reversible epigenetic control of the axis—contrasting with the germline layer, which the methylation results reported above show to be transcription-coupled but not disease-mediating and therefore not a useful intervention target. Low-toxicity candidates that engage this window are available in principle: the green-tea catechin epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) directly inhibits DNA methyltransferase 1 [24], and demethylating agents of the 5-azacytidine class provide clinical precedent [22, 23]. These results frame acquired epigenetic regulation of the C1GALT1–C1GALT1C1 module as the most concrete entry point for Gd-IgA1-lowering strategies (Fig. 6d), and provide a testable framework for functional validation.
### Independent cell-level cross-check: the chaperone module shifts along the B-cell maturation axis at single-cell resolution
To test the cell-state anchor at single-cell resolution, we reanalysed the IgAN peripheral-blood mononuclear-cell scRNA-seq dataset of Kim and colleagues (GSE285335; 26 samples — 6 late-stage and 11 early-stage IgAN, 9 healthy controls; 282,463 cells) [30]. Gating on cells expressing at least two of the four canonical B-cell markers (MS4A1, CD19, CD79A and CD79B) identified 42,259 B-lineage cells (14.96% of all cells) — a broad gate that captures B cells together with plasma and plasmablast-like cells, as defined by the marker rules below — of which 23,458 were naive (IGHD- or IGHM-positive), 2,403 memory (CD27-positive) and 16,398 plasma-marker-high (a combined XBP1 + PRDM1 + JCHAIN + MZB1 score above a low, prespecified log1p-CPM threshold; Supplementary Table S7 and Methods); this marker-gated B-lineage partition is reported separately from the unsupervised partition described below, which resolves the same B-lineage pool into a strict B-cell compartment and a plasma-cell compartment (Fig. S4). Along the naive-to-plasma axis, the C1GALT1 detection rate rose from 22.7% to 25.6% (Fisher exact P = 4.0 × 10⁻¹¹) and the C1GALT1C1 detection rate from 3.1% to 5.2% (Fisher exact P = 2.8 × 10⁻²⁵), whereas per-cell expression changed only modestly (mean non-zero log1p CPM: C1GALT1 1.18 → 1.05; C1GALT1C1 1.02 → 0.85; Mann–Whitney P = 2.9 × 10⁻⁷⁵ and 1.5 × 10⁻²⁵, respectively) (Supplementary Table S8 and Fig. S4). Because these per-cell tests treat individual cells as independent replicates and C1GALT1C1 is detected in only ≈ 4% of B cells, we repeated the contrast on donor-level pseudobulk units (n = 26 donors, each contributing both a naive and a plasma unit): the detection-rate shift was confirmed for both genes (C1GALT1 0.223 → 0.255, paired Wilcoxon P = 1.1 × 10⁻⁴; C1GALT1C1 0.032 → 0.052, P = 7.5 × 10⁻⁷), whereas the mean per-cell expression level was unchanged for C1GALT1 (P = 0.57) (Supplementary Table S8b). Because the broad B-lineage gate resolved a large plasma/plasmablast-like fraction (16,398 of 42,259 cells; 38.8%), in which droplet doublets are a recognised confounder, we also repeated the contrast after removing a transparent high-RNA doublet proxy (cells above the per-sample 99th percentile of detected-gene number; this class was enriched in the plasma-like compartment—odds ratio 3.5, Fisher P = 1.6 × 10⁻⁷⁰—but comprised only 3.8% of it). The donor-level detection-rate shift persisted for both genes (C1GALT1 paired Wilcoxon P = 1.8 × 10⁻³; C1GALT1C1 P = 2.3 × 10⁻⁵; Supplementary Table S8c), so the cell-state trend is not attributable to high-RNA droplets, although a dedicated doublet caller was not applied. Two further checks address the composition of this permissive gate (Supplementary Table S8d). First, the plasma-marker-high class retains the canonical B-cell markers—95.4% of its cells remained MS4A1-positive and 96.3% CD79A-positive, and only 2.2% carried all four plasma markers—so we read it as plasmablast-leaning rather than as a purified plasma-cell population. Second, the same contrast recomputed in the independent, marker-rich unsupervised partition of these cells (plasma identity defined from MZB1, JCHAIN, XBP1, DERL3, TNFRSF17 and CD38 rather than from the four-marker score) reproduced both detection-rate increases at compartment resolution (C1GALT1 21.4% → 24.5%, Fisher P = 6.7 × 10⁻¹³; C1GALT1C1 3.2% → 4.9%, P = 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁶). At the finest resolution of that partition—an argmax subdivision of the B/plasma compartment—the C1GALT1C1 shift was larger still (3.3% → 7.2%, P = 9.5 × 10⁻⁴¹) whereas the C1GALT1 difference was no longer significant (23.0% → 22.1%, P = 0.13), so the C1GALT1 component is read at compartment level only. Maturation therefore increases the proportion of cells in which the module is detectably expressed rather than the amount expressed per cell. Because the 10x 5′ assay captures low-abundance transcripts inefficiently (C1GALT1C1 detected in 4.1% of all B-lineage cells), we treat the detection-rate shift as the primary read-out and the per-cell decline as complementary (Methods).
### Upstream regulator inference for the axis is negative, whereas the C1GALT1C1 promoter carries methylation-blockable Sp/KLF motifs
Within the same atlas, we first resolved the cell states present in the peripheral-blood compartment. After removing technical genes (ribosomal, mitochondrial and immunoglobulin segments) from the highly variable set (2,000 → 1,726 genes), cells were embedded by truncated singular value decomposition (50 components), partitioned into 600 micro-clusters by MiniBatchKMeans, and clustered by Louvain community detection on the micro-cluster k-nearest-neighbour graph, yielding 15 communities annotated through a transparent, mutually exclusive marker gate (Supplementary Fig. S5). The compartment is dominated by T cells (58.3%) and NK cells (17.3%), with B cells at 5.3% (14,967 cells) and plasma cells at 9.6% (27,069 cells); the two compartments together comprise 42,036 B-lineage cells, the same pool that the marker-based gate above resolves (42,259), so the two partitions differ in how they split the B-lineage pool rather than in its size. Axis-gene expression was not B-cell-restricted: C1GALT1 was detected in 21.4% of B cells but at comparable rates in T (20.7%), NK (17.1%) and myeloid cells (18.4–25.6%), and C1GALT1C1—the most actionable member by our scoring—had the lowest detection rate of any lineage in B cells in this unsupervised atlas partition (3.2%); the marker-gated B-lineage partition described above gives a similar estimate (4.1%) (Fig. S4). This lineage distribution does not support B-cell-restricted transcription of the axis and reinforces the cell-type-precise intervention argument developed above. Consistent with the design of the source dataset, the atlas is T-cell-dominated (CD3E detected in 74% of cells; CD14 in 1.2%), and it is used here to characterise cell state and upstream regulation rather than to compare patients with controls; the unsupervised and marker-gated partitions of these data resolve the same B-lineage pool under different marker rules and therefore differ in absolute B-cell subtype counts (the marker-gated partition assigns more cells to the plasma-like compartment, whereas the unsupervised gate resolves a larger naive pool); both are reported (Fig. S4).
We next inferred upstream transcriptional regulators within the B-cell compartment. CollecTRI regulons were restricted to the expressed gene panel (8,248 genes; 778 regulators with ≥ 5 panel targets and no upper target-size limit), regulator activity was estimated per cell as the mean z-score of its targets (univariate linear model), and activity was related to a composite axis score across donors separately within each B-cell state (pseudobulk units of donor × B-cell state; 103 units), a method that controls for cell-type composition (Supplementary Fig. S6 and Table S10). The panel carries a strong global covariation of library complexity and shared transcriptional output, and random gene sets reproduce most of the apparent signal: target-number-matched random sets reached a median Spearman ρ of 0.67–0.88 against the axis score (95th percentile 0.82–0.91), which overlaps the observed regulon range (median 0.78; maximum 0.93). Significance was therefore judged against an empirical null matched to each regulator's own target number (1,000 random sets per distinct size; 179 sizes) with Benjamini–Hochberg control within each B-cell state, rather than by ρ alone. Only 84 of the 2,334 regulator-by-state combinations (3.6%) exceeded their size-matched null at empirical P < 0.05, at or below the 116.7 (5%) expected from the empirical null itself (one-sided binomial test for excess enrichment, P > 0.99; the observed count lies below rather than above the null expectation, so there is no evidence of enrichment), so the layer shows no global enrichment, and a single combination survived FDR control: ERF in naive B cells (18 targets; ρ = 0.93; empirical P < 0.001; q < 0.001), a factor with no role in the cytokine, methylation or Sp/KLF narrative and one that did not survive the global-covariate control analysis reported below (there it fell to q = 0.62). The regulators that motivated the analysis did not reproduce. In the pre-specified candidate set (STAT1, STAT3, STAT6, NFKB1, RELA, NFKB2, IRF1, IRF4, IRF8, JUN, JUNB, FOS, PRDM1, PAX5, EBF1, SPI1, SMAD3, DNMT1, KLF2 and MYC; 60 combinations) three combinations reached empirical P < 0.05 in naive B cells (SMAD3, ρ = 0.91, P = 0.014; JUN, ρ = 0.90, P = 0.012; MYC, ρ = 0.90, P = 0.048), exactly the 3.00 expected by chance, and none survived FDR control (q ≥ 0.76); STAT3 (ρ = 0.88), STAT6 (ρ = 0.86), NFKB1 (ρ = 0.89), RELA (ρ = 0.89), DNMT1 (ρ = 0.85), KLF2 (ρ = 0.84) and SP1 (ρ = 0.88) all lay inside their target-size-matched nulls (q ≈ 1.0). In a control analysis that additionally regressed the unit-level global covariate out of both sides, only 3 of the 2,334 combinations reached q < 0.10—all with ≤ 13 panel targets (PRRX1, SCX and HOXB13)—and the FDR-surviving regulator ERF dropped to q = 0.62, indicating that the naive correlation structure largely reflects the shared global covariate rather than regulator-specific coupling. We therefore report this layer as negative: in these data, no upstream regulator of the axis is distinguishable from a target-size-matched random gene set.
Because the significant axis CpGs identified above are gene-body marks, we asked which transcription-factor motifs could be blocked directly by methylation at the C1GALT1C1 promoter CpG island—the anchor of the actionable window identified above. Scanning the island (chrX:120,629,882–120,630,375; 493 bp; 56 CpG dinucleotides; GC content 66.9%) and the five significant C1GALT1 gene-body CpGs with the 879 non-redundant JASPAR 2024 CORE vertebrate motifs identified 242 methylation-sensitive region-by-factor associations involving 178 factors; 431 motif hits were recovered in the island, of which 261 overlapped a CpG dinucleotide and 56 (21.5%) belonged to its 21 Sp/KLF family members (Supplementary Table S9). This sequence-level enrichment is the one axis-centred regulatory observation that rests on genomic sequence alone, and it is consistent with the published finding that hypermethylation of the C1GALT1C1 (Cosmc) promoter lowers its expression [23]. It does not, however, convert into a data-driven claim. Intersecting the island motifs with the regulators that passed the target-size-matched null returned no factor at the pre-specified threshold (q < 0.10); the tolerated post hoc list—factors at empirical P < 0.05 or in the top 40 by ρ, namely ASCL1, E2F6, ETV6, FOXA2, HOXA9, MYC, MYCN, SMAD3, SNAI1, SP2, SP3, TCF12, TCF3 and ZNF148—does not survive FDR control. We therefore retain the cytokine–DNMT1–Sp/KLF circuit only as a literature-anchored hypothesis for the acquired epigenetic window, and not as a result of this study: IL-17 and IL-4 signalling acting through STAT3 and STAT6 would raise DNMT1, methylation of the C1GALT1C1 promoter would exclude CpG-centred factors including KLF2 and SP1, C1GALT1C1 transcription would fall, and galactosylation capacity would decrease. The hypothesis remains correlative and sequence-predicted rather than causal: the regulator associations are observational and, as shown above, no stronger than their matched random sets, the motif analysis is computational and unvalidated by chromatin or reporter assays, and no gene-perturbation experiment was performed (see Discussion and Limitations).

## Discussion

IgAN is thought to arise through a multi-hit cascade in which Gd-IgA1 acts as the initiating hit [3]. Gd-IgA1 is produced by the O-glycosylation machinery of B cells and plasma cells, and variants in the genes encoding this machinery—C1GALT1, C1GALT1C1 and GALNT12—have been reported for both Gd-IgA1 levels and IgAN risk, without the two phenotypes being distinguished [6, 7, 8]. We therefore separated the axis into testable layers: cell-type-specific cis-regulation, epigenetic control, the quantity of the intermediate trait, and disease susceptibility.

Across these layers, axis variants acted as cis-regulatory elements strongest in naive B cells (Fig. 2), reached genome-wide significance for Gd-IgA1 in Han Chinese (Fig. 3), and showed marked East Asian–European allele-frequency differentiation at C1GALT1 (Fig. 5a–b)—yet gave no support for a shared causal variant with IgAN and no association with susceptibility in the IgAN meta-analysis; germline methylation marked transcriptional coupling without mediating risk (Fig. 4).

Set against the multi-hit model, these results place the axis at the first hit as a determinant of a quantitative intermediate trait rather than a driver of susceptibility: the axis is associated with how much Gd-IgA1 an individual produces, not with whether that individual develops IgAN. This reconciles the strong Gd-IgA1 associations of these variants with their disease-negative results, and places the burden of disease conversion on downstream amplifiers—mucosal immunity and complement activation—that lie outside the axis and are not addressable by our design [3]. The boundary is informative in both directions: it argues against using axis genes as case–control markers, and it supports circulating Gd-IgA1 as the modifiable intermediate readout through which interventions on galactosylation capacity would be monitored.
Our result is concordant with an independent preprint that reached a similar conclusion by different means: Fung et al. [33] found, from association testing and Mendelian randomization, that Gd-IgA1 is more likely a marker than a cause of IgAN. Our framework reaches the same boundary from an orthogonal direction—cell-type-resolved cis-regulation, transcription-coupled methylation and cross-ancestry frequency structure—and adds three increments beyond it: resolution to the naive B-cell state, localisation of the signal to the epigenetic rather than the germline layer, and the East Asian frequency structure of the two Han-Chinese leads. We therefore conclude that this axis is unlikely to act as a susceptibility driver in the populations studied, while locating its modifiable component in acquired epigenetic control of the production phenotype.
The population dimension adds a second layer of specificity. Two of the four published Gd-IgA1 leads reached genome-wide significance in Han Chinese patients, and allele-frequency stratification shows marked East Asian–European differentiation at C1GALT1: the rs13226913-C allele approaches fixation in East Asians, and the rs10238682-G allele is approximately 2.6-fold more frequent than in non-Finnish Europeans (Fig. 5a–b); as noted above, these are the Gd-IgA1-lowering alleles at the locus, so the differentiation reflects the frequency of the non-risk allele rather than enrichment of the risk allele. This differentiation is not uniform, however — the GALNT12 lead rs7856182 is genome-wide significant for Gd-IgA1 in Han Chinese despite an East Asian frequency of only 0.025, indicating that strong effects can arise on rare local haplotypes rather than through a blanket "East Asian enrichment" pattern. Read together with the naive B-cell specificity described above, this places the axis signal at the intersection of a particular cell state and a particular population — Han Chinese patients, the group with the highest IgAN burden.

There is a further and less obvious consequence of this frequency structure. At rs13226913 the East Asian frequency of the Gd-IgA1-increasing T allele is only 0.074, so the locus carries very little common variation in the population with the highest IgAN burden: a variant that associates with galactosylation in Europeans has almost no genetic variance left to act on in East Asians, and its contribution to between-individual variation in Gd-IgA1 there must be correspondingly small. It is also the pattern expected if the near-fixed C allele rose under selection or simply drifted to fixation in East Asians, although allele-frequency data alone cannot separate the two. The same structure offers a parsimonious explanation for why the Han-Chinese Gd-IgA1 scan recovered its genome-wide significant signals at rs10238682 (East Asian frequency 0.522) and rs7856182 rather than at rs13226913: the informative intra-population signal at the C1GALT1 locus sits on the variants that still segregate in East Asians, whereas the European lead is close to monomorphic there.
Until East Asian, cell-type-resolved eQTL panels exist, trans-ancestry interpretation of axis variants will remain anchored to European reference data, and the East Asian signal we document here cannot be fully resolved into its regulatory components.
The single-cell analysis adds a cell-state dimension to the genetic anchor. Along the naive-to-plasma trajectory, the module does not behave as a uniformly up- or down-regulated unit: the proportion of cells detectably expressing C1GALT1 and C1GALT1C1 increases while per-cell expression changes little — an expression-spectral shift rather than graded up-regulation. Read together with the cis-eQTL results, this suggests that germline variation acts on the probability that a cell engages the module, whereas the per-cell output is set by other factors, a pattern consistent with a saturating, O-glycosylation-coupled regime for the chaperone, an interpretation that remains to be tested directly. It should be noted, however, that axis transcription was not confined to B cells in this dataset (C1GALT1 was detected at comparable rates in T, NK and myeloid cells, and C1GALT1C1 was least often detected in B cells), so the naive-B-cell localisation of the cis-eQTL describes the genetic control of the axis rather than a B-cell-restricted expression programme.
A further layer of cell-level analysis tested—and did not support—the idea that the axis sits within a concrete upstream circuit. Unsupervised atlas construction and regulator inference in the same IgAN PBMC dataset (Supplementary Figs. S5 and S6) located no regulator whose inferred activity exceeded a target-size-matched random gene set once the shared global covariation of the panel was taken into account: only 84 of 2,334 regulator-by-state combinations cleared that null at empirical P < 0.05 (3.6%, at or below the 116.7 expected from the null itself; no excess), a single combination survived FDR control (ERF in naive B cells, a factor unrelated to the axis), and the pre-specified cytokine-, methylation- and B-cell-identity candidates—including STAT3, STAT6, NFKB1/RELA, DNMT1 and KLF2—all fell inside their matched nulls. What survives from this layer is sequence-level rather than data-driven: the C1GALT1C1 promoter CpG island is densely populated by methylation-sensitive Sp/KLF motifs, including binding sites for KLF2 and SP1. Read together, the two analyses leave the cytokine–DNMT1–Sp/KLF circuit as a literature-anchored hypothesis rather than a finding of this study: cytokine signalling would raise DNMT1, promoter methylation would exclude Sp/KLF factors, and C1GALT1C1 transcription would fall. This is the mechanism that would connect the intervention window identified below to inflammatory input, and it is consistent with the cytokine-driven down-regulation of C1GALT1C1 reported in IgAN B cells [22, 23]; but it is not demonstrated here. The regulator analysis is inferential—the associations are observational and, as reported above, no stronger than their matched random sets—and the motif predictions were not validated by chromatin-accessibility, ChIP or reporter assays.
Finally, the intervention target lies in the acquired, potentially reversible epigenetic layer rather than in the germline: C1GALT1 methylation is transcription-coupled yet does not mediate risk (Fig. 4), and no axis member is an approved drug target, so direct enzyme inhibition is not a realistic lever. We therefore nominate — rather than rank — C1GALT1C1 as the candidate node, on the single criterion that it is the only axis member with IgAN-context functional evidence: cytokine-driven down-regulation (IL-17 [22], IL-4 [23]) and IL-4-driven promoter hypermethylation reversed by 5-aza-2′-deoxycytidine [23]. This gives the framework a two-node architecture rather than a single winner — C1GALT1 carries the genetic anchor (naive-B-cell cis-eQTL plus a genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 association) and C1GALT1C1 carries the intervenability anchor — and the two are stated separately because the evidence supporting them is of different kinds. Pharmacological precedent exists (EGCG [24]; the 5-azacytidine class [22, 23]; class I HDAC inhibition remains hypothesis-level, without direct IgAN evidence). Two caveats attach to this node itself: the methylation–expression coupling is correlational and cancer-type-dependent, and systemic up-regulation is cautioned against by the tumour-promoting activity reported for C1GALT1 [19, 20, 21], although direct tumour data for the chaperone itself point the other way: in mice, intestinal-epithelial deletion of Cosmc (C1GALT1C1) causes spontaneous colorectal adenocarcinoma [31], and pancreas-specific Cosmc loss accelerates Kras-driven pancreatic carcinogenesis [32]. Both rest on loss of function, so the tumour evidence for C1GALT1C1 favours restoring rather than inhibiting it; yet whether restoration raises C1GALT1 activity enough to offset that protection is untested. Either way, cell-type- and timing-precise approaches remain preferable. The hypothesised circuit makes falsifiable predictions: restoring C1GALT1C1 expression, or reversing promoter hypermethylation, should restore galactosylation capacity in patient B cells, and the predicted KLF2 and SP1 motifs should be occupancy-blocked by methylation. Testing these requires perturbation and chromatin-level experiments that public summary data cannot provide.

This is a causal-inference, not a causal-validation, framework: it bounds evidence from observational summary data and rests on no perturbation or reporter assay, so the disease-level nulls bound the shared-causal-variant evidence available from the current GWAS rather than excluding any relationship. The Han-Chinese Gd-IgA1 GWAS is released with P values only and the full-effect-size dataset (Kiryluk et al. [7]) was not accessed, so trans-ancestry heterogeneity and the mediation component could not be quantified, and the Mendelian randomization and colocalisation nulls are read as absence of shared-causal-variant evidence, not proof of absence — the eQTL–IgAN colocalisation in particular had ≤ 1.3% power to detect a shared variant with an effect at the upper end of those observed at the locus (Supplementary Fig. S8). The same limitation precluded the most direct colocalisation of all, between the axis cis-eQTL and the Gd-IgA1 GWAS itself, so the axis-to-Gd-IgA1 link rests on per-variant association and direction consistency rather than on a shared-causal-variant posterior. The regulatory panels interpreting the East Asian signal do not match the study population: OneK1K and GTEx are European, and chromosome X is absent from both the IgAN meta-analysis and OneK1K, leaving the C1GALT1C1 lead rs5910940 untestable in those two resources and non-significant where it is measurable (GTEx v10 whole blood and kidney cortex, P = 0.31 and 0.52); C1GALT1C1—the node nominated for intervention—therefore rests on functional and sequence-level evidence rather than on a significant cis-eQTL. The Japanese ImmuNexUT cross-reference [25] narrows but does not close this gap, since the effect-size comparison is by sign only. The disease-side inference is limited in the same direction: the IgAN meta-analysis we used is a predominantly European case–control sample (15,587 European-ancestry and 71 East-Asian-ancestry cases), so the disease-negative result is effectively a European-ancestry result and is not powered to exclude an axis contribution to IgAN risk in East Asians. An ancestry-matched Asian-only meta-analysis (4,590 biopsy-diagnosed cases and 7,573 controls) likewise showed no genome-wide significant association for any testable autosomal lead, although it remains smaller than the European analysis and a nominal association at rs10238682 (P = 0.040) does not survive correction. The methylation input is incomplete in the positive direction: GoDMC reports significant variant–CpG pairs rather than exhaustive cis scans, so the shared-variant signal at cg17994788 rests on a curated variant subset and requires independent replication in a full per-variant mQTL scan. The transcriptomic and single-cell anchors are likewise directional, resting on one series with two pooled controls (GSE73953) and on a 10x 5′ assay detecting C1GALT1C1 in only ≈4% of B cells, which places the chaperone near the detection limit of that assay; the maturation-axis read-out is therefore based on detection rate, confirmed at the donor level by pseudobulk analysis, and absolute per-cell abundances are not interpreted. The broad B-lineage marker gate additionally resolves a larger plasma/plasmablast-like fraction (38.8%) than is typical of PBMC, in which droplet doublets are a recognised confounder; the maturation-axis result survived removal of a high-RNA doublet proxy (Supplementary Table S8c), but no dedicated doublet caller was applied, so a residual doublet contribution to the plasma-like gate cannot be excluded. The gate is permissive rather than a purified plasma-cell definition — 95.4% of the plasma-marker-high cells retain MS4A1 and only 2.2% carry all four plasma markers (Supplementary Table S8d) — so the maturation axis is read as a cell-state association within the B-lineage and not as a quantitative plasma-cell contrast, and the C1GALT1 component of the shift is resolved at compartment level rather than at the finest sub-partition of the independent unsupervised partition (Table S8d). Finally, the upstream regulatory layer (Supplementary Figs. S5 and S6) is both inferential and negative: regulator activities were inferred rather than measured and did not exceed a target-size-matched null, so no regulator–axis coupling is claimed; the only element of that layer that survives is a sequence-level prediction—the density of methylation-blockable Sp/KLF motifs in the C1GALT1C1 promoter island—which no reporter, ChIP or perturbation assay in our design tests.

Nine layers of evidence, assembled entirely from public data and including an independent cell-level cross-check, show that the IgA1 O-glycosylation biosynthesis axis is under cell-type-specific genetic control concentrated in the naive B-cell state and transcription-coupled epigenetic regulation, and that this machinery shapes the circulating level of Gd-IgA1 without a detectable shared causal variant for IgAN susceptibility; the upstream-regulator layer, by contrast, returned a negative result, so that the promoter-level Sp/KLF occupancy at C1GALT1C1 remains a sequence-level prediction rather than an inferred circuit. The absence of a shared causal structure between the axis and disease risk—reproduced across cell-type-resolved, methylation and disease-level analyses—supports a multi-hit view in which the first hit, excess Gd-IgA1, is genetically separable from the subsequent hits that convert it into disease. It also refocuses the clinical value of the axis on Gd-IgA1 as the modifiable readout of an intervention window located in reversible acquired epigenetic control, which may be most consequential in East Asian populations, where the two genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 associations in our panel were discovered and the disease burden is highest. Re-analysis with full-effect-size Gd-IgA1 data and validation in East Asian, cell-type-resolved regulatory panels are the logical next steps.

## Methods

### Study design and data sources
This observational study is a secondary analysis of publicly available, de-identified transcriptomic and genome-wide summary data; no new clinical or experimental data were generated, and ethics approval and consent for publication were not applicable. We built a layered causal-inference framework (Fig. 1c) to dissect the genetic and epigenetic control of the IgA1 O-glycosylation biosynthesis axis—comprising the core 1 β1,3-galactosyltransferase C1GALT1, its molecular chaperone C1GALT1C1 (COSMC), the sialyltransferase ST6GALNAC2 and the GalNAc-transferases GALNT2 and GALNT12—in IgAN. The five-gene panel was pre-specified from the genes reported by the prior quantitative Gd-IgA1 GWAS [6, 7, 8], rather than selected genome-wide from the wider GALNT (20 members) and ST6GALNAC (6 members) families; this is a deliberate anchoring choice and the remaining family members were not systematically screened (see Discussion). All datasets, versions and accessions are listed in Table 1. Disease transcriptomes were obtained from three Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) series: GSE73953 (peripheral blood mononuclear cells [PBMC]; IgAN n = 15 versus pooled healthy controls n = 2; Agilent GPL4133), GSE115857 (kidney biopsy bulk tissue; IgAN n = 55 versus living-donor controls n = 7; Illumina HumanHT-12 DASL, GPL14951) and GSE93798 (microdissected glomeruli; IgAN n = 20 versus controls n = 22; Affymetrix GPL22945). Cell-type-resolved and tissue eQTLs were taken from the eQTL Catalogue release 8 [12], which contains uniformly recomputed summary statistics for OneK1K single-cell B-cell subtypes (naive, memory and intermediate; 876, 726 and 618 donors, respectively) [10] and for GTEx version 10 whole blood (n = 853) and kidney cortex (n = 106) [9]. For disease susceptibility we used the IgAN meta-analysis of Sakaue et al. (GWAS Catalog GCST90018866) [11]; its cases are overwhelmingly of European ancestry (15,587 European-ancestry and 71 East-Asian-ancestry cases; Table 1); for the Gd-IgA1 production phenotype we used the Han-Chinese quantitative Gd-IgA1 GWAS of Wang et al. (GCST90011884), whose public release contains association P values only [8]. Whole-blood methylation QTLs (mQTLs) were queried from the Genetics of DNA Methylation Consortium (GoDMC; n = 24,988–28,181 per CpG) [13]. Allele frequencies were extracted from the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD) version 4 [14], and druggability annotation from Pharos/IDG (Illuminating the Druggable Genome) [15].
### Differential expression analysis
For each GEO series, probe-level expression was mapped to the five axis genes (multi-probe genes were collapsed by taking the mean per gene). Differential expression between IgAN and the corresponding control group was assessed on the log2 scale with Welch's two-sample t-test (unequal variances; scipy.stats.ttest_ind with equal_var = False), and effect sizes are reported as log2 fold change (logFC). Because the analysis was hypothesis-driven on a pre-specified five-gene panel, we report nominal P values in the main text and provide complete probe-level results (including the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate across all measured probes where applicable) in the supplement. The PBMC series served as the primary directional (exploratory) analysis because it captures the circulating B-cell/plasma-cell compartment that synthesises Gd-IgA1; the kidney series served as directional-contrast datasets. The two pooled healthy controls of GSE73953 limit the PBMC analysis to a directional inference, and the cDNA-mediated annealing, selection, extension and ligation (DASL) platform of GSE115857 has a narrow dynamic range; both limitations were prespecified before analysis. As an independent cell-level cross-check, we reanalysed the IgAN peripheral-blood mononuclear-cell (PBMC) single-cell RNA-seq dataset of Kim and colleagues (GSE285335; 26 donors: 6 late-stage IgAN, 11 early-stage IgAN and 9 healthy controls; 10x Genomics 5′ gene-expression assay; 282,463 single cells); details are given in the Single-cell B-cell reanalysis subsection below.
### eQTL context mapping
For each of the five axis genes, cis-eQTL evidence was screened across five immune/tissue contexts derived from eQTL Catalogue release 8 [12]: OneK1K B naive, B memory and B intermediate [10], and GTEx v10 whole blood and kidney cortex [9]. Context measurability was assessed with the uniformly recomputed permutation-based gene-level significance (p_perm) and the nominal P of the lead cis-variant (p_beta). Three categories of "context non-measurability" were recorded as genuine data features rather than technical failures: (i) C1GALT1C1 resides on chromosome X, which is not represented in the OneK1K eQTL release; (ii) GALNT12 and ST6GALNAC2 show very low expression in B-cell subtypes and are filtered by the discovery pipeline; and (iii) a given gene might be measurable in bulk tissue but not in purified cell types.
### Replication of published Gd-IgA1 lead variants
Four published Gd-IgA1 lead variants—rs13226913 and rs10238682 at C1GALT1, rs7856182 at GALNT12 and rs5910940 at C1GALT1C1—reported in European [6, 7] and Han-Chinese [8] Gd-IgA1 GWAS, were looked up in the same eQTL contexts as above. Replication was declared when the lead variant showed a significant cis-eQTL in at least one OneK1K B-cell context; GTEx v10 whole blood and kidney cortex served as corroborating tissues. rs5910940 (chromosome X) is not testable in the OneK1K release and was carried forward with the corresponding limitation. To test formally whether the C1GALT1 lead effect varies with B-cell state, the per-subset effect estimates and standard errors from the uniformly recomputed OneK1K summary statistics were combined in an inverse-variance-weighted (IVW) fixed-effect meta-analysis, heterogeneity across the three B-cell states was tested by Cochran's Q (2 degrees of freedom, with the I² statistic), and the contrast between the naive and antigen-experienced states (memory and intermediate) was evaluated by weighted least-squares meta-regression of the subset effect on a naive-versus-experienced indicator. The three per-subset estimates derive from the same donors and the same genotypes and are therefore not independent; because the contrast variance is computed under an independence assumption, whereas a positive between-subset correlation reduces the true variance of the difference and therefore makes that independence-assumption variance an over-estimate, the resulting contrast test is conservative.
### Colocalisation and fine-mapping
To test whether the cis-eQTL signal of the axis shares a causal variant with IgAN susceptibility, we performed colocalisation between the eQTL signal of each gene and the IgAN GWAS (GCST90018866) [11] in each context in which the gene was measurable (5 genes × 5 contexts; 25 planned comparisons). Colocalisation used the approximate Bayes factor (ABF) approach of Giambartolomei et al. [16], reimplemented in Python line-by-line against the official R coloc (≥ v5) source code and validated with synthetic data. Priors were set to p1 = p2 = 1 × 10⁻⁴ and p12 = 1 × 10⁻⁵, with a shared prior effect variance W = 0.04 (a prior standard deviation of 0.2, within the range conventional for cis-regulatory effect sizes) and aligned effect alleles between eQTL and GWAS beta signs; all priors were fixed before analysis and held identical across the 25 planned comparisons. Posterior probability of hypothesis 4 (PPH4) ≥ 0.8 was defined as strong and 0.5–0.8 as suggestive colocalisation. To calibrate how informative each null result is, we added a simulation-based power analysis at the *C1GALT1* locus: for each context a single causal variant was placed at the eQTL top variant, eQTL and GWAS summary statistics were then drawn from the observed per-variant standard errors over a grid of true shared-variant effects with the eQTL true effect fixed at its observed value, and PPH4 was recomputed with the same implementation and the same priors (4,000 replicates per scenario; Supplementary Fig. S8 and Tables S16a and S16b). Because ABF colocalisation assumes a single causal variant per region, comparisons in which the eQTL Catalogue provides official SuSiE credible sets [17] were re-tested at the signal level: for each SuSiE independent signal, the signal-level log-ABF was reconstructed from the credible-set member summary statistics and combined with the identical IgAN marginal ABF background used in the region-level analysis, with a sensitivity analysis at p12 = 5 × 10⁻⁶ (the coloc.susie default). One colocalisation that our design could not perform is the most direct of all—between the axis cis-eQTL and the Gd-IgA1 GWAS itself—because the public Han-Chinese Gd-IgA1 release contains association P values only and the full-effect-size European dataset (Kiryluk et al. [7]) is dbGaP-controlled; the axis-to-Gd-IgA1 link therefore rests on per-variant association and direction consistency rather than on a shared-causal-variant posterior (see Discussion).
### Allelic scoring and direction consistency
To summarise the joint direction of the axis on IgAN susceptibility, the three autosomal leads with available IgAN beta and standard error (rs13226913, rs10238682, rs7856182) were combined by an unweighted allelic score (sum of betas divided by the square root of the summed variances, two-sided normal test) [18]. Because rs13226913 and rs10238682 lie ~8.2 kb apart and are in linkage disequilibrium, this score is not an instrument-variable analysis with independent instruments and is reported strictly as an informal direction-consistency summary; formal inference rests on the per-variant P values and the region-level colocalisation described above. Direction consistency between the effect of each lead on cis-expression and on Gd-IgA1 was described qualitatively from published effect directions [6, 8].
### Two-step methylation mediation framework
To test whether DNA methylation marks or mediates the genetic-to-transcriptional step of the axis, we implemented the feasible components of a two-step Mendelian randomization (MR) framework (genetic variant → CpG methylation → IgAN) [18] with publicly available whole-blood mQTLs. cis-mQTL associations for the two interpretable autosomal C1GALT1 lead variants (rs13226913 and rs10238682) were queried from GoDMC (GRCh37) [13]; significant cis-CpGs were defined at P < 5 × 10⁻⁸. The GoDMC resource is a curated catalogue that reports significant variant–CpG pairs rather than exhaustive per-variant cis scans: the CpG-centred queries used here returned 193–817 variant records each, against the 1,736–5,205 variants covered by the corresponding eQTL windows. The mQTL input is therefore complete for identifying significant CpGs but not for variant-level enumeration, so the mQTL-based colocalisations—including the positive result at cg17994788—are reported as requiring independent replication. CpG-to-IgAN causal effects were estimated by (i) the Wald ratio using the lead variant and, independently, the top cis-sentinel variant of each CpG as instruments, and (ii) inverse-variance weighting over LD-clumped cis instruments where ≥ 3 independent instruments were available, all against the IgAN outcome (GCST90018866) [11]. Colocalisation was performed between (i) mQTL and IgAN, and (ii) mQTL and the C1GALT1 cis-eQTL in GTEx v10 whole blood and kidney cortex and in OneK1K B naive, using the same ABF implementation and priors as above. Direction coupling between methylation and expression was assessed by aligning the mQTL and eQTL effects of the lead variant to the same effect allele. A CpG was classified as a promoter mark when it fell within TSS ± 1.5 kb; gene-body and 3′-flanking CpGs were interpreted as transcription-coupled gene-body methylation marks. Because the Gd-IgA1 GWAS release contains P values only [8], formal estimation of the proportion of the genetic effect on Gd-IgA1 mediated by methylation was not feasible, and the mediation component is therefore reported as a qualitative convergence of the input, mediation and direction layers (see Limitations).
### Direct lookup of axis variants in the IgAN GWAS
To test directly whether the axis carries any disease-association signal, two pre-specified look-ups were performed in the IgAN meta-analysis (GCST90018866) [11]. First, the four published Gd-IgA1 lead variants were read out individually. Second, for each of the five axis genes, the top cis-eQTL variant (lowest nominal p_beta) in every pre-specified primary context in which the gene was measurable—the naive-B-cell and the two bulk-tissue contexts (10 gene–context pairs)—was looked up regionally; the antigen-experienced B-cell contexts were not part of the pre-specified look-up, and the X-chromosomal C1GALT1C1 records are untestable in the meta-analysis. Variants were selected on the basis of their cis-eQTL evidence only and were never filtered or prioritised on the basis of their IgAN association. P values are reported without additional multiple-testing correction because the look-up is a targeted check of a fixed, pre-specified set of variants rather than a genome-wide scan; the complete lookup is tabulated in Supplementary Tables S4a and S4b. Reported without correction by design, the look-up was additionally subjected to a Benjamini–Hochberg sensitivity analysis across the 13 testable look-ups (three evaluable lead variants plus the ten gene–context window look-ups): the lead-variant look-ups remained null (smallest adjusted q = 0.12), and the only look-up reaching q < 0.05 was the GALNT12 regional window minimum (rs187211065, effect-allele frequency 0.0024, P = 9.4 × 10⁻⁶, q = 6.1 × 10⁻⁵) — a rare-variant window scan that remains approximately two orders of magnitude above the genome-wide significance threshold (P = 9.4 × 10⁻⁶ versus 5 × 10⁻⁸) and does not involve any axis lead variant. Both look-ups were additionally repeated in an ancestry-matched Asian-only IgAN meta-analysis (4,590 biopsy-diagnosed cases and 7,573 controls; GWAS Catalog GCST90308724), which contains chromosomes 1–22 only; the full ancestry-matched result set is given in Supplementary Table S14.
### East Asian heterogeneity and allele frequency
Population-layer inference combined three lines of evidence. First, association P values of the four published leads in the Han-Chinese Gd-IgA1 GWAS [8] were compared with those in the IgAN meta-analysis [11]. Second, effect-allele frequencies in East Asian (EAS) and non-Finnish European (NFE) populations were extracted from gnomAD v4 [14]. Third, because the Chinese Gd-IgA1 release is P-value-only (no effect sizes), a formal trans-ancestry heterogeneity Q test was not possible; heterogeneity inference therefore rests on the convergence of the P-value distribution, the negative disease outcome and the allele-frequency stratification, stated explicitly rather than as a computed statistic. The X-chromosomal lead (rs5910940) is absent from the IgAN meta-analysis (no chromosome X), and its population-layer evidence is correspondingly incomplete. For the three autosomal leads, these frequencies were independently corroborated in a Chinese population reference panel (Westlake Biobank for Chinese, WBBC; 4,480 individuals; V20210103).
### Druggability and intervenability scoring
Direct druggability of the five axis genes was annotated from Pharos/IDG target development levels (Tclin, approved drug target; Tchem, potent small-molecule ligand; Tbio, characterised biology; Tdark, understudied) [15]. Because enzyme-active-site inhibition was not supported by the annotation, we scored the transcriptomic/epigenetic intervention window of each gene with a prespecified, expert-opinion semi-quantitative framework — the intervenability score (intervention or target-engagement potential) = G(0–3) + D(0–3) + F(0–2) − P(0–2) — in which each dimension was graded against explicit anchors. The score is a transparent ranking aid that encodes expert judgement rather than a data-derived estimate, and it is not weighted equally with the data layers above. G (genetic/regulatory anchor to the Gd-IgA1 production phenotype) scored 3 for a lead variant with a genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 association (P ≤ 1 × 10⁻⁸) corroborated by cis-eQTL replication in ≥ 1 B-cell context, 2 for a nominal Gd-IgA1 association or a B-cell cis-eQTL alone, 1 for transcriptomic or literature-based evidence only and 0 in the absence of any anchor. Because this anchor is defined jointly on the association and the B-cell cis-eQTL, a lead that is genome-wide significant for Gd-IgA1 but has no measurable B-cell cis-eQTL—because its gene is not expressed in B cells (GALNT12)—scores G = 2 rather than G = 3. D (availability of an epigenetic drug handle on the gene) scored 3 for direct functional evidence in IgAN B cells (e.g. reversal of C1GALT1C1 promoter hypermethylation by the demethylating agent 5-aza-2′-deoxycytidine), 2 for cross-disease functional evidence or gene-selective research-grade ligands, 1 for cross-disease correlational or hypothesis-level evidence and 0.5 for theoretical accessibility without a tested compound. F (functional and in vivo pharmacological evidence in IgAN or related models) scored 2 for direct in vitro or in vivo evidence in IgAN-relevant cells or models, 1 for cross-disease or non-IgAN evidence and 0 otherwise. P (0–2) penalised specificity, safety and off-target risks, including systemic toxicity of nucleoside demethylating agents, broad-spectrum HDAC inhibitor toxicity, the tumour-promoting activity reported for C1GALT1 [19, 20, 21] and broad sialylation inhibition. Intermediate half-points (0.5, 1.5, 2.5) were permitted between adjacent anchors of G and D. Criteria were prespecified before scoring, and item-level justifications for each gene are tabulated in the supplement. Scores are used solely for ranking and visualisation and make no causal claim. Literature anchors for the acquired epigenetic window included the cytokine-driven hypermethylation of the C1GALT1C1 promoter (IL-17 down-regulates C1GALT1 and C1GALT1C1, with expression restored by 5-azacytidine [22]; IL-4 likewise down-regulates both genes, in its case through promoter CpG-island hypermethylation, reversed by the demethylating agent 5-aza-2′-deoxycytidine [23]) and the direct binding and inhibition of DNA methyltransferase 1 by the green-tea catechin epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) [24], which we reference strictly as published evidence independent of our own functional work.
### East Asian regulatory cross-reference (independent ancestry check)
Most of the regulatory panels used above (OneK1K, GTEx) are of European origin, whereas the two genome-wide significant Gd-IgA1 associations in our panel were discovered in Han Chinese patients (Results); this ancestry mismatch is the principal interpretive limitation of the regulatory layers. To provide a population-matched regulatory cross-reference, we queried the ImmuNexUT resource [25], a Japanese immune-cell-type-specific cis-eQTL catalogue built from 416 individuals across 28 purified peripheral-blood subsets, including five B-lineage subsets (naive, switched-memory, unswitched-memory and double-negative B cells, and plasmablasts). Conditional eQTL summary statistics (independent signals after forward–backward conditional analysis at a gene-level FDR < 0.05) were obtained from the National Bioscience Database Center (NBDC) Human Database (accession E-GEAD-398; GRCh38); the presence of an axis gene in a subset's conditional file identifies it as a significant eGene in that subset. For the two C1GALT1 leads (rs13226913, chr7:7207215; rs10238682, chr7:7215386), we additionally recorded whether the variant itself was the forward-selected independent signal (association rank 0) for C1GALT1 in each subset, together with its nominal P value and forward effect slope. Because ImmuNexUT and OneK1K are independent cohorts, the comparison is a cross-ancestry direction-consistency check rather than a formal trans-ancestry replication, and effect sizes are compared by sign only. The lead-variant slope is lineage-dependent (positive in all B-lineage subsets in which rs10238682 is detected, but of opposite sign in neutrophils and low-density granulocytes), so only the B-lineage direction is used in the comparison. This cross-reference informs the East Asian analysis and the ancestry caveats of the Limitations. The complete axis-gene cross-reference and the per-lead C1GALT1 eQTL detail are provided in Supplementary Tables S12 and S13.
### Single-cell B-cell reanalysis (independent cell-level cross-check)
To corroborate the B-cell-state specificity of the cis-eQTL signal at single-cell resolution and in an IgAN-disease context independent of the European eQTL panels, we reanalysed the IgAN PBMC single-cell RNA-seq dataset of Kim and colleagues (GSE285335), a 10x Genomics 5′ gene-expression dataset comprising 26 donors (6 late-stage IgAN, 11 early-stage IgAN and 9 healthy controls; 282,463 cells). Per-sample 10x count matrices (CellRanger-filtered barcodes, features and matrix triplets) were downloaded from the GEO supplementary archive, loaded with scipy.io.mmread and merged by column with scipy.sparse.hstack, yielding a single genes-by-cells sparse matrix. Per-cell library sizes were computed as the column sums and a minimum library size of 500 counts was applied; because the deposited matrices are already CellRanger-filtered, this threshold excluded no further cells, and all 282,463 cells (minimum library size 501 counts) were retained for analysis. The five axis genes and a marker panel of B-cell subtype markers (MS4A1, CD19, CD79A, CD79B, IGHD, IGHM, CD27, XBP1, PRDM1, JCHAIN, MZB1) were extracted from the integer sparse matrix and normalised per cell to counts-per-million (CPM) followed by log1p. To control for cell-type composition rather than to embed the data in a low-dimensional manifold, we used marker-based classification rather than unsupervised clustering. B-lineage cells were defined as cells expressing at least two of the four canonical B-cell markers (MS4A1, CD19, CD79A, CD79B) at log1p CPM > 0 (n = 42,259 cells, 14.96% of the total; this broad gate captures plasma and plasmablast-like cells in addition to B cells, and is referred to below as the marker-gated B-lineage partition). B-cell subsets were defined on the basis of marker positivity, with the rules applied in a fixed order — the B-lineage gate first, and the plasma-marker rule taking precedence over the naive and memory rules wherever a cell satisfied more than one of them: naive (IGHD+ or IGHM+, CD27−), memory (CD27+, IGHD−/IGHM−) and plasma-marker-high/plasmablast-leaning (a single combined threshold on the summed log1p CPM of XBP1, PRDM1, JCHAIN and MZB1, ≥ log1p(3), rather than individual positivity for all four markers); markers with near-zero detection in 10x 5′ PBMC data (SDC1, AICDA) were excluded to avoid false-negative bias. C1GALT1 and C1GALT1C1 expression in each B-cell subset was quantified by two complementary read-outs: (i) the detection rate (fraction of cells with log1p CPM > 0) compared between subsets with Fisher's exact test, and (ii) the expression level among expressing cells, compared with the Mann–Whitney U test. Because the per-cell read-outs treat individual cells as independent replicates, the naive-versus-plasma contrast was additionally evaluated on donor-level pseudobulk units (one value per donor and B-cell state), with the donor as the unit of replication and a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test; this donor-level analysis is reported alongside the per-cell tests. Group comparison among B cells of late-stage, early-stage and healthy donors was performed with the same two read-outs and is reported in full in the reproducibility package; because that contrast addresses disease staging rather than the B-cell-state specificity examined here, and the dataset is not powered for clinical staging, it is not carried into the main text. Because 10x 5′ PBMC has a low per-cell capture rate for low-abundance transcripts (C1GALT1C1 is detected in ≈ 4% of B cells), the detection-rate read-out is reported as the more reliable single-cell signal in this data type. The full analysis script, per-subset counts and the test statistics are provided in the reproducibility package (reproducibility/code/directional_validation/ and Supplementary Tables S7, S8, S8b and S8d). The output of this reanalysis is presented as exploratory, independent corroboration of the cell-state-specific eQTL signal reported above; it is not a quantitative estimate of the genetic effect size. As a robustness check for droplet doublets in the broad B-lineage gate, the trajectory was repeated after removing a transparent high-RNA doublet proxy (the top 1% of cells by detected-gene number within each sample) and the outcome is delivered as Supplementary Table S8c; a dedicated doublet caller was not applied. Two further audits of the composition of this gate are delivered as Supplementary Table S8d (script step6_bcell_gate_audit.py): its marker composition — 95.4% of the plasma-marker-high cells remained positive for MS4A1 and 96.3% for CD79A, whereas only 2.2% carried all four plasma markers, so the class is read as plasmablast-leaning rather than as a purified plasma-cell population; and the B-lineage maturation contrast recomputed under the independent, marker-rich unsupervised partition of the same cells at both of its resolutions (compartment level, cell_type B versus Plasma; and its B_naive versus B_plasma sub-partition).
### Single-cell atlas and upstream regulator inference
To resolve the cell states and upstream regulators of the axis, the merged GSE285335 count matrix (26 samples; 282,463 cells after the library-size filter described above) was re-processed without prior annotation. Highly variable genes were selected on the per-cell normalised matrix (2,000 genes) and technical genes—ribosomal and mitochondrial transcripts, MALAT1, NEAT1 and immunoglobulin variable segments—were removed, leaving 1,726 genes. Cells were embedded by truncated singular value decomposition (50 components) on z-scored values, partitioned into 600 micro-clusters with MiniBatchKMeans, and clustered by Louvain community detection (resolution 2) on a k-nearest-neighbour graph of the micro-cluster centroids, yielding 15 communities. Communities were annotated by a prespecified, mutually exclusive marker gate applied in a fixed order (natural killer (NK): NKG7 or GNLY; T: CD3D; B: MS4A1 and CD79A; monocyte: CD14 or LYZ; dendritic cell: FCER1A; plasmacytoid dendritic cell: LILRA4; plasma cell: MZB1 or JCHAIN; platelet: PPBP; haematopoietic progenitor: CD34), so that no cell could receive more than one label; the gate is applied to cluster-level marker expression rather than by per-cell argmax assignment, which avoids the spurious enrichment produced by unsupervised clustering of a T-cell-dominated dataset. Regulator activity was inferred from the CollecTRI regulon resource [26] restricted to the expressed panel (778 regulators with ≥ 5 panel targets; no upper target-size limit); the per-cell activity of each regulator was computed with decoupleR's univariate linear model (ULM) [27], whose per-cell test statistic is the mean z-score of the regulon's target genes, and a composite axis score was computed from the z-scored axis genes. To control for cell-type composition, activity was aggregated to pseudobulk units of (donor × B-cell state) within the B-cell compartment and related to the composite axis score across donors by Spearman correlation. Because random gene sets attain high ρ against the composite score through the shared global covariation of the panel (median ρ 0.67–0.88, which brackets the observed regulon range), significance was assessed against an empirical null matched to each regulator's own target number—1,000 random gene sets drawn from the expressed panel for each distinct regulon size—and the empirical P value was the fraction of null sets with an equally or more extreme ρ; these P values were then corrected for multiple testing by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure within each B-cell state, and FDR-derived q values are reported. A set of 20 regulators with a priori relevance to the axis (cytokine-downstream factors, DNA-methylation factors and B-cell-identity factors; listed in Results) was pre-specified for descriptive reporting only and was not used to set the significance threshold. A control analysis repeated the test after linearly regressing the unit-level global covariate (the mean z-score of all panel genes within a unit) out of both the regulator activity and the composite score, applying the same size-matched null to the residualised values.
### Methylation-sensitive motif analysis at axis CpGs
To identify transcription-factor motifs that could be blocked directly by DNA methylation, sequences were retrieved for the five significant C1GALT1 gene-body CpGs (GRCh37 coordinates ± 60 bp) and for the C1GALT1C1 promoter CpG island (GRCh38 chrX:120,629,882–120,630,375). The 879 non-redundant JASPAR 2024 CORE vertebrate position-frequency matrices [28] were converted to log-odds position weight matrices with a 0.25 pseudocount and scanned at a per-motif threshold of 80% of the maximum attainable score; hits whose window overlapped a CpG dinucleotide were classified as methylation-sensitive. The resulting region-by-factor associations were intersected with the regulators that passed the size-matched empirical null at a pre-specified FDR threshold of q < 0.10; factors ranking in the top 40 by ρ or reaching empirical P < 0.05 are additionally reported as a tolerated post hoc list.
### Statistical software and versions
All analyses were performed in Python (version 3.13.14) with NumPy, pandas, SciPy and Matplotlib. Differential expression used Welch's t-test as implemented in scipy.stats. Colocalisation used our validated pure-Python implementation of the coloc ABF method [16], numerically checked against R coloc (≥ v5); signal-level re-testing used eQTL Catalogue official SuSiE credible sets [17]. An R+limma equivalence script (lmFit/eBayes/topTable) is provided for independent reproduction of the differential-expression step. Multiple-testing interpretation was layer-specific: nominal P values for the five-gene hypothesis-driven expression panel, permutation-based p_perm from the uniformly recomputed eQTL Catalogue for the eQTL layers, and region-level Bayesian posteriors for the colocalisation layers. Significance thresholds were set within each layer and were not combined into a single family-wise error rate across the nine evidence layers; the framework is therefore an assembly of layer-specific inferences rather than a single joint test, and no cross-layer combined significance is claimed. A full software and data-manifest is provided in the reproducibility package.
